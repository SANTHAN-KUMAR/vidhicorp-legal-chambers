## Where things stand

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 received assent in August 2023. It does not commence in one step: Section 1(2) allows different provisions to be brought into force on different dates, and the substantive machinery depends on rules notified by the Central Government. Businesses should treat the commencement date as a deadline they do not control, and work backwards from it.

## What the Act asks of a business

Any organisation that determines the purpose and means of processing digital personal data is a **Data Fiduciary**, with obligations that include:

- processing only for a lawful purpose, on consent or on a specified legitimate use;
- giving a clear, itemised notice at or before the point of collection, available in English and the languages in the Eighth Schedule;
- maintaining accuracy and completeness where the data is used to make a decision affecting the individual;
- reasonable security safeguards, and breach notification to the Board and to affected individuals;
- erasing personal data when consent is withdrawn or the purpose is served;
- enabling access, correction, erasure and grievance-redressal rights, and publishing the contact details of a Data Protection Officer or responsible person;
- heightened obligations for **Significant Data Fiduciaries**, once notified, including a DPO based in India, independent data audit and periodic data protection impact assessment.

Penalties are substantial and are assessed against the fiduciary.

## Why the timing is tight

The obligations are not difficult to state. They are difficult to satisfy without knowing what personal data the business holds, where it sits, who it was shared with and on what basis it was collected. In most mid-sized Indian companies that answer does not exist in written form, and assembling it across HR, sales, marketing, vendor management and any customer-facing application is a multi-month exercise.

The organisations that struggle at commencement will be the ones that started drafting a privacy notice before they finished the data map.

## What to do now

- **Map the data.** One inventory: what personal data is collected, by which system, for what purpose, retained how long, shared with whom.
- **Fix the consent trail.** Consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, with a clear affirmative action. Pre-ticked boxes and bundled consent do not qualify.
- **Look at your processors.** Processing through a vendor is permitted only under a valid contract; the fiduciary stays accountable. Your existing vendor agreements almost certainly do not carry the necessary terms.
- **Handle employee data deliberately.** Employment data is not outside the Act; parts of it fall under legitimate uses, but the boundary needs to be drawn on the facts.
- **Build the rights process** for access, correction and erasure, and name the person who owns it.
