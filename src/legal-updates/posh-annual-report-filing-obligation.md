## What the law requires

Under Section 21 of the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013, the Internal Committee must prepare an **annual report** in the prescribed form and submit it to the employer and to the **District Officer**. Section 22 requires the employer to include in its annual report the number of cases filed and disposed of during the year.

For companies, the Companies (Accounts) Rules require a statement in the **Board's Report** that the company has complied with the provisions relating to the constitution of an Internal Committee under the Act.

## Why it matters

Most organisations we see have done the visible half of POSH compliance — a policy on the intranet, an IC constituted, an annual training session — and none of the filing half. The annual report to the District Officer is the obligation most often missed entirely, and it is the one that leaves a documentary gap that is difficult to cure later.

The consequence is not only the penalty for non-compliance. Where a complaint is subsequently litigated, the absence of filings is used to argue that the committee was constituted on paper.

## What to check before the year closes

- **Is the IC validly constituted?** A presiding officer who is a woman employed at a senior level, at least two members from among employees committed to the cause or with relevant experience, and **one external member** from an NGO or association familiar with the issues. At least half the members must be women.
- **Has the annual report been filed** with the District Officer for each completed year, and is the acknowledgement on record?
- **Does the Board's Report carry the statement?** Check the last two years, not just the current one.
- **Are the registers and minutes maintained** so that a complaint, its enquiry and its disposal can be traced?
- **Have branch offices been covered?** An IC is required at every office or administrative unit, and an employer with fewer than ten workers falls under the Local Committee instead.
