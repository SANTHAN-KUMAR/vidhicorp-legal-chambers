## What changed

Section 12A of the Commercial Courts Act, 2015 requires a party to exhaust pre-institution mediation before instituting a commercial suit, **unless the suit contemplates urgent interim relief**. In *Patil Automation Private Limited v. Rakheja Engineers Private Limited*, the Supreme Court held that this requirement is mandatory, not directory, and that a plaint filed in breach of it is liable to be rejected under Order VII Rule 11 of the Code of Civil Procedure.

## Why it matters to a business

Commercial disputes — recovery of dues, breach of a supply or distribution agreement, a shareholder dispute above the specified value — are routinely filed in a hurry. Skipping mediation to save four weeks can cost the entire suit, along with the limitation period consumed while the plaint sat on the file.

The urgent-interim-relief carve-out is also narrower in practice than it looks. Pleading urgency purely to bypass mediation invites scrutiny, and courts have examined whether the urgency asserted is genuine.

## What to do

- Before issuing a demand notice on a commercial matter, decide honestly whether the case genuinely needs interim protection — an injunction, an attachment, an order preserving assets or accounts.
- If it does not, initiate pre-institution mediation through the authority notified under the Legal Services Authorities Act and keep the record of it. The non-starter report is itself a document you will file.
- Build the mediation window into the recovery timeline rather than discovering it at the drafting stage. Where limitation is close, this is a planning decision, not a procedural footnote.
- Where a contract already contains a tiered dispute-resolution clause, check that it does not conflict with the statutory step.
