## What changed

Clause (h) of Section 43B of the Income-tax Act, 1961 provides that any sum payable to a **micro or small enterprise** registered under the MSMED Act, 2006 is deductible only in the year in which it is actually paid, unless it is paid within the time limit specified in Section 15 of the MSMED Act. That limit is **45 days** where there is a written agreement, and **15 days** where there is none.

The proviso that allows other Section 43B items to be claimed if paid before the return filing date does **not** rescue a delayed MSME payment.

## Why it matters to a business

This converts a working-capital practice into a tax cost. A buyer that habitually pays small suppliers at 60 or 90 days now carries the deduction into a later year, inflating current-year taxable income. The exposure sits with the buyer, not the supplier, and it is picked up at assessment from the auditor's disclosures.

Two further points are frequently missed:

- The provision applies to **micro and small** enterprises. Medium enterprises are outside it.
- It turns on the supplier's **registration status**, which the buyer often does not track.

## What to do

- Collect and refresh Udyam registration details from your vendor master, and tag each vendor micro, small, medium or unregistered. This is a data exercise before it is a legal one.
- Review your standard purchase order and vendor agreement templates. Where credit terms exceed 45 days for a registered micro or small supplier, the clause is now working against you.
- Align the accounts-payable ageing report to flag MSME dues separately, so the exposure is visible before year end rather than at audit.
- Note the separate disclosure obligations on MSME dues in the financial statements and in the half-yearly MSME return.

We regularly review vendor contract templates and payment terms alongside compliance teams. If your supplier base includes a meaningful number of small enterprises, this is worth a look before the next year end.
