<p style="margin-bottom:0"><strong>Key takeaways:</strong> Size the ESOP pool against an 18–24 month forward hiring plan, not current headcount — typically 8–15% of the fully diluted cap table at early stage in India. Know before a term sheet arrives that investors often ask for pool top-ups to be created pre-money, concentrating dilution on founders. Build in an evergreen mechanism so unvested, forfeited options return to the pool rather than eroding it permanently.</p>
</div>

<p>An employee stock option pool is easy to treat as a housekeeping item — a percentage set aside, approved by the board, and revisited only when it runs low. Indian startups that treat ESOP pool sizing this way tend to find themselves negotiating a pool top-up during a funding round, at exactly the moment they have the least leverage to do it on their own terms, with a term sheet clock running and an investor's counsel already pushing for pre-money dilution.</p>

<h2>The legal mechanics of ESOPs in India, briefly</h2>
<p>For private companies, ESOPs are governed by the Companies Act, 2013 and the Companies (Share Capital and Debentures) Rules, 2014, which set out requirements including board and shareholder approval for the scheme itself (and for each grant, in practice, through the scheme's operating framework), a minimum one-year vesting cliff between grant and first vesting, and restrictions on grants to promoters and certain directors — subject to the DPIIT-recognised-startup carve-out that permits such grants for a defined window after incorporation. None of this is unusual or particularly difficult to comply with procedurally — the harder questions around ESOP pool sizing in India are commercial, not procedural, and they're the ones founders tend to under-plan for.</p>

<h2>Sizing questions worth answering before the pool exists</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>What does the pool need to cover over the next 18–24 months</strong>, not just current headcount? A pool sized for today's team is already undersized by the time the next hiring round happens, particularly for a company scaling engineering or sales headcount quickly after a raise.</li>
<li><strong>Who absorbs the dilution when the pool is topped up?</strong> Investors frequently negotiate for a pool increase to be sized pre-money — meaning the dilution from the top-up falls on existing shareholders, largely founders, rather than being shared proportionately with the incoming investor. Understanding this dynamic before a term sheet arrives changes how the pool should be planned for in earlier rounds, since a founder who anticipates this can negotiate a larger initial pool on better terms than a founder negotiating a mid-round top-up under time pressure.</li>
<li><strong>Is the pool being quietly shrunk in a down round?</strong> A pool that isn't refreshed at each round, or that gets diluted alongside everyone else in a down round, can leave a company without enough allocated equity to make competitive offers exactly when retention matters most — often the moment a company can least afford attrition.</li>
<li><strong>What happens to unvested options when someone leaves?</strong> A scheme with a clear mechanism for cancelled or forfeited options to return to the pool (an "evergreen" structure) avoids the pool being permanently eroded by ordinary attrition over several years of hiring and departures.</li>
<li><strong>Standard vesting terms, and whether they suit this specific company.</strong> A four-year vesting schedule with a one-year cliff is the common market standard in India, though the details — acceleration on a change of control (single-trigger vs double-trigger), treatment on termination without cause, treatment on a founder's own departure — are negotiable and worth deciding deliberately rather than defaulting to a template pulled from an unrelated deal.</li>
<li><strong>DPIIT recognition status.</strong> Whether the company is a DPIIT-recognised startup affects whether promoters and certain directors can participate in the scheme at all, and for how long after incorporation — a threshold question worth confirming before assuming founders will be included in pool allocations.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Why ESOP structuring is a legal conversation, not only an HR one</h2>
<p>Pool sizing sits at the intersection of the cap table, the next fundraising round's term sheet, and the Companies Act's procedural requirements for approving and amending the scheme — which is why getting it structured correctly from the outset, with proper board and shareholder resolutions, a clearly drafted scheme document, and individual grant letters that actually match the scheme terms, tends to save both money and negotiating leverage later. A scheme patched together under time pressure during a raise is a common source of cap-table disputes and diligence findings in later rounds.</p>

<h2>What a well-structured Indian ESOP scheme document should actually cover</h2>
<p>Beyond the headline pool size and vesting schedule, a scheme worth relying on typically addresses: the exercise price mechanism and any minimum exercise price requirements, the exercise window following vesting and following termination of employment, treatment of options on a change of control or IPO, tax withholding obligations at exercise (perquisite tax under Indian income tax law falls on the employee at exercise, with a further capital gains event on eventual sale), and the board's discretion — and its limits — to amend the scheme going forward. Founders who leave these points to be resolved ad hoc, grant by grant, tend to create inconsistency across the option pool that surfaces as a diligence issue at the worst possible time — during a fundraise or an acquisition.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3>What's a typical ESOP pool size for an early-stage startup in India?</h3>
<p>Ranges vary by stage and hiring plans, but pools in the broad range of 8–15% of the fully diluted cap table are common at early stages in the Indian startup ecosystem, sized against a forward hiring plan rather than current headcount alone. Later-stage companies sometimes run smaller ongoing top-ups, refreshed at each round rather than sized once at the outset.</p>
<h3>Who pays for an ESOP pool top-up during a funding round?</h3>
<p>This is a heavily negotiated point. Investors commonly ask for the pool increase to be created pre-money, which concentrates the resulting dilution on existing shareholders — largely the founders — rather than sharing it proportionately with the incoming investor. Understanding this dynamic before a term sheet arrives changes how the pool is planned for in earlier rounds, and is worth raising explicitly during term sheet negotiation rather than discovering in the definitive documents.</p>
<h3>What is the minimum vesting period for ESOPs under Indian law?</h3>
<p>The Companies (Share Capital and Debentures) Rules, 2014 require a minimum period of one year between the grant of options and the date they first vest. Beyond that statutory minimum, the total vesting schedule (commonly four years, with the first year as a cliff) and any acceleration terms are matters of company and scheme design, not statutory mandate.</p>
<h3>Can promoters and directors of an Indian private company receive ESOP grants?</h3>
<p>The Companies (Share Capital and Debentures) Rules restrict ESOP grants to promoters and to directors who hold more than 10% of the equity shares of the company, subject to specific exceptions — notably a carve-out for startups recognised by the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), which are permitted to issue ESOPs to promoters and such directors for a defined period after incorporation. Confirming DPIIT recognition status is therefore a relevant threshold question before assuming founders can be included in the scheme.</p>
<h3>What happens to unvested ESOP options when an employee leaves?</h3>
<p>This depends on the scheme's own rules, but a common and well-advised structure is for unvested options to lapse on exit and return to the pool for future grants — sometimes called an evergreen mechanism. Vested but unexercised options are typically given a defined post-exit exercise window under the scheme document; without a clear evergreen mechanism, a pool can be permanently eroded by attrition even though it looks adequately sized on paper.</p>
